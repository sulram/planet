# VISION

The horizon. Prose on purpose: this file carries the why. Nothing here is a
commitment; commitments live in ROADMAP.md.

## A planet in a number

A whole planet, thousands of square kilometres of mountains, caves and oceans,
fits in a seed. The seed is the DNA, the generator is the physics, and the world
is not stored: it is remembered identically by every machine that looks at it.
Nobody downloads the planet. Everyone recomputes it and arrives at the same
place.

So the database becomes something else: the record of presence. Everything
that is nature comes from noise. Everything in the file is somebody's trace.
The world file is an archaeological site.

## What it is

A finite, spherical world where people and AI agents walk, fly, drive and
build together. It blends two lineages: Cryptovoxels, with its blocks, in-world
building and land, and Hyperfy, with its GLBs, entities and scripts. Nature is
smooth and organic; human architecture stands out in crisp cubes.

It is large enough to feel flat on foot. You only notice the sphere from far
away: from a tower a hint, from flight a curve, from orbit a planet. There is a
sun, and because the sphere is real, there are time zones. There is a moon.
There are rockets and satellites you can build, and you can fly like Superman.

## A world is a world

planet is not a platform. It is one kind of world among those mundos hosts:
mundos knows who each person is, gives the world its address and its version,
and keeps it running. The world knows none of that. It has no accounts, no
administration and no list of other worlds; it has a recipe, a folder with
what people did, and a door.

A new world is empty of everything, even of a planet. Its first admin walks
the candidates, an Earth or a seed's own continents, and founds it from the
one on the screen. From then on anyone may enter, with no account at all, and
those the world trusts build.

One engine opens any world that speaks its language. Today that is a browser
tab, handed the engine by the world itself. Further out it is a headset paired
with a code on a screen, visiting one world after another, and for that each
world says what it speaks.

## A small core, and layers that switch

The core is the sphere: a planet from a number, a body that walks, flies and
swims on it, a sky, a sea, and other people there. Everything done on it is a
layer over that, a plugin: speaking, the body one wears, building, land,
media. A layer is switched on or off for a world, and two ways of building can
stand side by side until one of them proves itself.

The core stays small on purpose. A small core is what can be wrapped again: by
a web page today, by a desktop app, by a headset, by a front end written all
in Rust. What is decided once, and well, in the core is what every one of
those inherits.

## One landlord, many hands

The world is finite, and land is the layer that says so. A landlord draws
volumes on the map and names who may build there; they carve smaller volumes
for others. Outside any volume, nature is protected. The map opens slowly,
starting from a dense city, with the rest left as wilderness to explore. Until
that layer exists, a world trusts its admins and its builders everywhere.

Everything anyone does is recorded, and therefore reversible. Freedom with a
trail.

## Macro and micro

Honey, I Shrunk the Kids; the land of giants; A Bug's Life: all in the same
world. Scale is a property of the avatar. A brick in someone's wall can hold a
kingdom, with its own landlord and its own gravity. These secrets are real: the
server simply never sends the small world to those who are too big to see it.
To find them you need magic, or to know where the portals are.

## Light enough for everything

The same client runs on a desktop, in a browser tab, on a Raspberry Pi and
inside a headset. The network carries only what people made; untouched nature
costs nothing. Images and videos load as you approach them, under a strict
budget, so a gallery of a hundred videos does not kill the scene.

## Free software, end to end

wgpu, Rust, Go, SQLite. No proprietary engine underneath. This matters for
public culture: for MUSEU.XYZ as a Ponto de Cultura, and for public funding
that asks for free software.

## Further out

Wallets as another way to be someone. A wallet is who you are in the
metaverse: a door in mundos, the body you wear, and what you hold shown as
yours, the avatars and the galleries of OBJKTs from the old Hic et Nunc days. Land deeds as tokens, if that ever serves the community. The recipe of
the planet inscribed in Bitcoin: the complete genesis of a world in a few dozen
bytes, verifiable forever, and periodic snapshots anchored to the timechain as
the world's notary.

A cinema mode for exhibitions: the world rendered in the cloud with neural
rendering and streamed to any screen.

## Lineage

The shape came from Bowerbyte's *Blocky Planet: Making Minecraft Spherical*
(August 2025, `https://bowerbyte.com/posts/blocky-planet/`): a quad sphere of
six sectors, shells that keep a block's width as you go down, an address that
reads sector, shell, chunk, block, neighbours resolved across the seams, and
gravity that points at the centre. We arrived at most of that independently
and then found it written down, which is the useful kind of reference: it
confirms the geometry and it shows what a planet small enough to need no LOD
lets you skip.

October 2025: an idea for managed Hyperfy hosting, born from installing it by
hand on a VPS. September 2026: the idea turned over completely. Not hosting
someone else's metaverse: building our own, from zero, on our own terms.
October 2026: both turned out to be true. The hosting became mundos, which
runs Hyperfy for the workshops and holds the sign in, the roles and the
addresses; and the world built from zero became one of the worlds it hosts,
free to be only a world.
