# 78. A build stands on a platform of built cells, and the ground stays as it is (decided)

Logged 2026-09-30.

Nothing a builder does changes the ground. A volume holds the ground of its
plot and the air over it, and what a build stands on is a platform, laid
where the body stands: a slab of cells one thick, 8, 16, 32 or 64 a side as
picked in the panel, cut by the address as a plot is, its top over the highest
ground under it, on pillars down to the ground. Its cells are cells like any
other, in the paint in hand, and laying one is a stroke that undo takes back.
Taking a tool where no volume stands lays the first under the body, and a
button lays the next. A volume has no floor of its own, its chunks are cut by
the address along `h` too, and grass gives way under what is built.

**Why not the flatten.** Marlus saw 75's stamp at work on a hillside: a plot
held flat takes the slope with it 32 m at a time, a cut at the back and a fill
at the front, and every volume opened does it again. Nature is not the
builder's to change (58). A slab on pillars lets a mountainside carry a build
and keeps the mountainside.

**Why over the highest ground.** It was laid at the height of the feet first,
which Marlus picked from a drawing. In the world the hill came through the
slab and took a bite out of its edge, and he asked for the highest part. A
build needs the whole of its slab, so the top stands over the highest ground
at any corner of any column under it, and the body is lifted onto it.

**Why the side is picked.** Pillars are as tall as the drop under them, so on
a steep slope a slab of 32 m stands on towers and one of 4 m hugs the ground.
Only the builder knows which the place wants. The pick shows before building
starts, since starting lays the first platform.

**Why cut by the address.** For the reason plots are (77): platforms of one
size tile, two side by side meet edge to edge with their pillars paired, and
where a body happened to stand leaves no mark on the shape of what is built.

**Why cells.** A platform that is cells needs nothing new: it is painted,
deleted, taken back, drawn and walked on by what a stroke already has. What
an op carries for it is a square and a height (M3), and the cells follow from
the ground of the recipe.

**Why a volume has no floor.** The floor of 75 was the plane the stamp held.
With the ground as nature made it there is no plane: what is not a cell is
air, the ground is the generator's, and the client joins the two, a footing
being the higher of them and a line of sight stopping at the first. With no
floor to pass through the middle of a chunk, a chunk is cut by the address
along `h` as along `u` and `v`, and a stored chunk is named by its address
alone.

**Why the grass gives way.** A tuft stands up to a metre tall and comes up
through a slab laid on the ground. Marlus wanted the ground left as it is,
grass and all, and no worked earth under a build. A tuft is cosmetic, so it
is the tuft that yields: one that a cell stands in the way of is not grown,
and the patches around a change grow theirs again.

This takes the seat of a volume from 75, and the shared floor and the
terraces from 77. The rest of both stands. The stamp stays in the generator
for what is not a volume, a cave mouth first (58).

**Rejected:** the flatten for a volume; a choice between the two at opening;
the ground choosing by its slope; the slab at the height of the feet, giving
way where the ground comes through; worked earth under a platform, or over
the plot; a platform around the body, which leaves two of them askew; one
size for every platform; a floor kept at the bottom of a volume.
